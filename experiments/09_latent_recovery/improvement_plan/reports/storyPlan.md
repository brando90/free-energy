## 1. Core claim (professor's framing, sharpened)

**The single sentence the paper defends:** *Using an audited perturb-and-validate protocol — every planted step verified false against the rule closure, every continuation formally validated — we show that whether a language model absorbs, flags, or routes around a false step planted in its own proof trace is strongly modulated by the availability of refuting evidence in the local proof context, not by falsity alone: falsehoods refutable within one inference hop are flagged and routed around, while falsehoods refutable only by global closure search are silently reused in downstream derivation.*

The protocol itself is contribution #0 (the professor explicitly praised it: it "avoid[s] the common weakness in CoT-intervention work, i.e., treating arbitrary perturbations as 'errors' without proving they are actually false or checking whether the continuation is logically valid"). The three decisive pieces of evidence, in order of presentation:

1. **The matched audited sweep** (EXPA, n=150/position/condition, 1800 validated rows): globally-checkable falsehoods yield closure-valid 0.360–0.447, injection-dependence 0.233–0.420, doubt ≤0.020; one-hop falsehoods yield closure-valid 0.633–0.740, injection-dependence ~0, doubt 0.267–0.467; true interruptions and benign paraphrases sit at 0.75–0.92 with zero injection-dependence. Same generator, same problems, same positions — only distance-to-refuting-evidence varies.
2. **The distance-k dose-response** (Figure 1): negating the fact derived k hops downstream places refuting evidence exactly k inferences away; doubt falls monotonically (0.392→0.323→0.231→…→~0 at k=∞) and closure-valid declines then plateaus (k≥3); the 32B curve shifts up and compresses without removing the distance dependence — a parametric, within-family manipulation of the claimed variable.
3. **The paired local-certificate flip** (EXPB, 900 continuations, 183 problem clusters, strictly paired): inserting a true refuting sentence immediately before the *identical* global falsehood, against a length-matched irrelevant-certificate control, moves doubt 0.023→0.380 and injection-dependence 0.240→0.027, while closure-valid completion does *not* improve (0.500→0.467) — local counterevidence causally controls flagging and absorption but does not buy proof repair. (Stated against the irrelevant-certificate arm, the correct comparison; the current draft leads with Δ-vs-baseline, which overstates.)

Optional fourth leg (recommended, pending a powered rerun): the doubt-forcing pilot shows the deficit is one of *initiating* the check, not inability to use the evidence — a single forced "Wait" recovers most of the damage (0.286→0.714 closure-valid) at zero cost on true statements.

## 6. Title + abstract draft (TMLR version)

**Title (recommend keeping current):** "Nearby Evidence Shapes How Transformers Respond to Planted Errors in Their Own Reasoning."
Alternates: (a) "Flagged or Silently Reused: Local Counterevidence Shapes How Language Models Respond to Planted Errors in Their Own Proofs" — restores the memorable dichotomy while staying calibrated; (b) "An Audited Perturb-and-Validate Protocol for Planted Errors in LLM Reasoning: Local Counterevidence Modulates Absorption and Flagging" — foregrounds the methodological contribution (most TMLR-native).

**Abstract draft (~235 words):**

> Can a language model recover when a step in its own reasoning trace is wrong? Compounding-error arguments assume it cannot: one false step and the derivation is lost. We test the premise with an audited perturb-and-validate protocol: take multi-hop deduction problems the model already solves with a formally valid proof, overwrite one intermediate step of its own trace with a planted sentence, resume generation with no feedback, and formally validate every continuation against the task's rule closure. Auditing matters: 92% of "wrong" statements from a naive off-path generator are in fact entailed-true, and correcting this inverts the apparent result. On verified falsehoods, the response is organized by the distance to refuting evidence rather than by falsity alone. In the expanded Qwen2.5-7B sweep (150 instances per position per condition), falsehoods refutable one hop away yield closure-valid completion of 0.63–0.74 with verbalized doubt up to 0.47, while falsehoods refutable only through global closure search yield 0.36–0.45, near-zero doubt, and enter downstream derivation in 0.23–0.42 of continuations. Moving the refuting evidence k hops away produces a monotone decline in doubt and validity (7B and 32B); inserting a single refuting sentence before the same lie — against a length-matched irrelevant-certificate control — raises doubt from 0.02 to 0.38 and cuts injection-dependence from 0.24 to 0.03, without improving closure-valid completion. On tasks without redundant derivation paths, propagation is near-total (0.88–0.99), bounding the phenomenon's scope. All rates are upper bounds on a doubly filtered cohort; we release the validator, truth-status audit, and locked result artifacts.

(If EXPD lands, replace the certificate sentence's tail with: "…and a single forced hesitation token recovers most of the lost validity (0.29→0.71), locating the bottleneck at check initiation rather than checking ability.")
